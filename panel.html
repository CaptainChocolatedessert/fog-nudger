Connecting…

Reading the map happens on its own surface: it draws the map, paints the ink over it, and carries the controls that decide what counts as ink. It has to be its own surface because the answer here is a per-pixel verdict, which only a picture can carry — and because the controls belong beside the thing they change rather than in a panel the picture would cover.

Changing anything here re-reads the image and re-partitions the map, so it discards everything from tabs 2 and 3. What is here? reports what the last trace computed at the centre of your view — region, ink, or discarded floor, with the luminance it read.

How ink becomes regions

Neither control can split or join a region — the reading already decided which rooms exist. The minimum deletes a region and lets whatever encloses it absorb the space; simplification smooths every boundary, capped below the point where one could cross a wall. Both regenerate every polygon, so both discard hand edits made in tab 3. The reading is left alone, so this skips the slow half of a trace.

Staged proposals are GM-only and produce no walls, so nothing here can affect play until you accept. Accepting moves them to the fog layer, where Dynamic Fog starts deriving walls. Going back to staging is the exact inverse and keeps every hand edit; re-tracing does not.

How proposals look while you judge them

Neighbouring regions are drawn in different colours so the partition is what you see rather than a flat tint. Appearance affects staged proposals only — an accepted shape is forced to full opacity, because fog below that leaves a tint over ground the party has revealed.

Diagnostics

Workspace probe puts up an opaque full-screen sheet that keeps its own input, which is what stage one would eventually be drawn on. Drag on the pad, spin the wheel, right-click, and type in the box; the sheet reports whether Owlbear moved underneath, which you cannot see, because the sheet is covering it. …framed is the same thing inside Owlbear's own modal card, to find out what that frame costs. Both last 60 seconds and close on Escape, on their own button, or on their own timer — three ways out, because an opaque sheet is a worse thing to be stuck behind than a transparent one.
